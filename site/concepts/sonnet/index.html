yap is dictation for macos. there's no app to switch to and no window. a little blob sits on the edge of your screen, opens into a listening panel while you talk, and um your words appear and shrink as they pile up. this page is built the same way.

or double-tap it.

or click the blob to start and click it again to paste. hold ⌘ and drag it to move it to another edge.

yap uses about a tenth of the memory wispr flow did

1.3 gb

i wrote it to replace wispr flow, which sat on 1.3 gb of ram and about 5% cpu whether i was talking or not.

0.1 gb

yap sits on about 0.1 gb and only does anything while you're talking.

nothing leaves your mac.

it uses the speech model that comes with macos, so the audio stays here and it's free. transcripts are kept for 30 days, then deleted. recordings are off unless you turn them on.

settings › privacy shows how much is kept, and lets you stop saving what you say, change how long things are kept, or delete the lot.

it learns your words.

when you start talking it reads the window you're in and takes the names and odd words from it. say it with Redis on screen and the speech model hears “reddis”, but yap writes Redis, because it's one letter off exactly one word it knows. after a few times it learns the swap for good.

it never touches an ordinary word, and it doesn't keep what it read. basically words you keep deleting, like “basically”, get dropped. the plain rules take out um, uh, repeated words and a comma'd “like” or “you know”, so something like um “the the build is fine” comes out as “the build is fine”.

you can add your own to ~/.config/yap/words.txt, one per line:

LangWatch
btw => by the way
drop: basically

it isn't the most accurate one, and i'd rather say so.

nobody hand-checked these, so each one's scored against what the others agreed on, which is fine for comparing them but isn't an exact accuracy.

wispr flow cleans up filler with an llm. when i tried apple's on-device model for it, it deleted real words (“i really don't like”, “looks terrible”), and qwen3 4b wanted 2.9 gb and another 1.5 s, so yap takes fillers out with plain rules. none of them took out a real word on the same 40 clips.

settings has three levels of cleanup. light takes out um, uh and stumbles only. medium, the default, also fixes punctuation and grammar a sentence at a time, keeping your words. heavy also drops what you take back, so “send it to john, no wait, sarah” becomes “send it to Sarah”, and turns lists into bullets.

other apps can listen in.

yap says what it hears, as it hears it, on ~/.config/yap/live.sock: one line of json per change, with the words it's sure of, the ones it's still guessing, how loud you are, and the app it'll paste into. only your user can connect. rush uses it to show the dictation in its message box while you talk.

an app can tell yap which words to listen for by writing a line back, when it connects and whenever its words change:

{"context":{"app":"com.mitchellh.ghostty","words":["Redis","rushd","LangWatch"]}}

app is the bundle id of the terminal it runs in, or "" if it doesn't know. up to 200 words; lines over 64 kib and anything else are ignored.
