it lives on the edge of your screen

there's no app to switch to and no window. a little blob sits on the edge of your screen, like the one on the edge of this page. hold fn and it opens into a panel, your words come in as you say them and shrink as they pile up, and when you let go they're pasted where you were typing.

or click the blob to start and click it again to paste. hold ⌘ and drag it to move it to another edge. all of that works on this page too, with space for fn.

yap takes fn over while it's running, so the emoji picker and apple's dictation don't open. your setting comes back when you quit.

i wrote it to replace wispr flow

wispr flow sat on 1.3 gb of ram and about 5% cpu whether i was talking or not. yap sits on about 0.1 gb and only does anything while you're talking.

it learns your words

when you start talking it reads the window you're in and takes the names and odd words from it: anything with capitals in the middle, digits, or that the dictionary doesn't know. it tells the speech model to listen for them. if it then hears something that's one letter or so off exactly one of them, like "reddis" with Redis on screen, it fixes it, and after a few times it learns the swap for good. it never touches an ordinary word, and it doesn't keep what it read.

hold space now and watch: the words on this page it would pick up get marked while you talk.

words you keep deleting, like "basically", get dropped. settings › words shows what it's learned. you can add your own to ~/.config/yap/words.txt, one per line:

LangWatch
btw => by the way
drop: basically

this page has that file loaded, so try saying "btw" or "basically".

nothing leaves your mac

it uses apple's on-device speechtranscriber, the speech model that comes with macos, in any language it knows, from british english to brazilian portuguese.

transcripts are kept for 30 days, then deleted. recordings are off unless you turn them on. settings › privacy shows how much is kept and lets you stop saving what you say, change how long things are kept, or delete the lot.

this page is different. if you press "use my mic", your browser does the listening, and chrome, for one, sends that audio to google. the app doesn't send anything anywhere.

is it any good

so it isn't the most accurate. nobody hand-checked these, so each one's scored against what the others agreed on, which is fine for comparing them but isn't an exact accuracy. the panel on this page waits 0.9 s after you let go, so you can feel that number.

tidying up

there are three levels in settings. light is yap's own rules: um and uh out, and stumbles like "the the". medium, the default, also has apple intelligence fix punctuation and grammar on your mac, a sentence at a time while you talk, and keeps your words. i caught it deleting real words ("i really don't like" lost "really"), so yap only takes its answer if every word it took out was a filler.

heavy also drops what you take back, so "send it to john, no wait, sarah" becomes "send it to Sarah", and it turns lists into bullets. this page only does light.

it tells other apps what it hears

yap says what it hears, as it hears it, on ~/.config/yap/live.sock: one line of json per change, with the words it's sure of, the ones it's still guessing, how loud you are, and the app it'll paste into. only your user can connect. rush uses it to show the dictation in its message box while you talk.
